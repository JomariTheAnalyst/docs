This page records the payment-provider comparison used for the SixthGear online checkout and physical-store payment decision.

## Management Decision Snapshot

<Warning>
  **Maya is only conditionally recommended.** Management approval is limited to obtaining a written quotation and running a controlled online and physical-store pilot. A full rollout is **not approved**, and Maya's price advantage has **not been proven** because its final contracted rates, VAT treatment, QR Ph pricing, settlement charges, and other commercial terms remain unconfirmed.
</Warning>

| Decision area | Current management position |
| --- | --- |
| Online Shopify payments | Request a Maya Checkout quotation and compare the total contracted cost against PayMongo and Xendit before changing the live gateway. |
| Physical-store payments | Pilot Maya Terminal at one counter as an external terminal recorded through approved Shopify POS custom payment types. |
| One provider for both | Potentially simpler vendor management, support, settlement reporting, and escalation, but only if both channels meet cost, reliability, and reconciliation requirements. |
| Full rollout | Not approved. Requires a signed quotation, successful pilot, finance reconciliation, and management sign-off. |

## Rate status and limits

**Public rates verified:** July 28, 2026

The tables below use public provider pages and planning assumptions. They are not SixthGear's contracted rates.

- A merchant quotation can differ because of payment volume, merchant category, risk review, taxes, settlement terms, and negotiated discounts.
- PayMongo publishes its standard rates as exclusive of VAT.
- Xendit's pricing calculator displays VAT as a separate charge.
- Maya's public pages do not clearly establish the VAT treatment of every rack rate.
- Shopify may charge its own third-party transaction fee in addition to the payment provider's fee.
- Any tax added by Shopify to its own billing invoice is not included in the examples below and must be verified from the actual Shopify bill.

<Warning>
  Treat every calculation below as an **example only**. Do not use it as SixthGear's approved budget, actual Shopify plan, actual sales forecast, or provider quotation.
</Warning>

## Shopify fee added to online gateway payments

When a third-party provider processes an online Shopify payment, Shopify can charge a separate third-party transaction fee.

| Shopify plan | Shopify third-party transaction fee |
| --- | --: |
| Basic | 2.00% |
| Grow | 1.00% |
| Advanced | 0.60% |
| Plus | 0.20%, subject to the current Plus contract |

The examples on this page use **Shopify Basic at 2.00% only as a planning assumption**. This does not confirm SixthGear's actual plan.

Shopify states that third-party transaction fees apply to third-party and alternate online gateways. Shopify POS transactions and approved manual payment methods are treated differently. A payment processed separately through Maya Terminal and recorded as a Shopify POS custom payment type still incurs Maya's merchant charge, but it is not an online gateway payment.

## Decision by payment channel

### Online Shopify payments

Maya Checkout is approved only for quotation and controlled testing. Before changing the live online gateway, management must compare:

- Provider charge by payment method
- VAT treatment
- Shopify third-party transaction fee
- Plugin or partner fees
- Refund and dispute costs
- Failed-attempt charges
- Settlement schedule and bank-transfer charges
- Reconciliation reports and support process

PayMongo remains a valid online comparison. Xendit must be recalculated using its effective September, October, and December pricing.

### Physical-store payments

Maya Terminal is approved only for a one-counter pilot. It will operate as an **external payment terminal**, not a Shopify-integrated Philippine card reader.

Staff must complete the terminal authorization before marking the Shopify POS order paid. Shopify will not automatically receive Maya's authorization, settlement, refund, or chargeback details.

### Potential benefit of one provider

Using Maya for both channels could provide:

- One commercial relationship
- Fewer support and escalation contacts
- More consistent settlement reporting
- Easier comparison of online and physical-store fees
- Potential combined-volume pricing

These are possible operational benefits, not proven savings. A single provider should not be selected if its contracted total cost, availability, support, or reconciliation is weaker than using separate providers.

## Measurable pilot pass criteria

The payment pilot passes only when every required condition below is supported by evidence.

| Pilot measure | Pass condition | Required evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Card transactions | 100% of the approved card test cases are authorized for the correct amount, recorded once in Shopify POS, and matched to a provider reference | Shopify orders, terminal records, receipts, and reconciliation sheet |
| QR transactions | 100% of the approved Maya QR and QR Ph test cases are confirmed through the provider record, not a customer screenshot, and recorded once in Shopify POS | Provider dashboard or terminal confirmation and Shopify order |
| Refund testing | The approved full-refund test completes correctly; partial-refund behavior is also tested when the contracted service supports it | Original sale, refund reference, Shopify record, and settlement impact |
| Reconciliation variance | **₱0.00 unexplained variance** between Shopify, provider records, fees, refunds, and the bank settlement | Signed daily reconciliation |
| Settlement timing | The net settlement reaches the approved bank account within the schedule stated in the written quotation | Provider settlement report and bank credit date |
| Finance visibility | Finance can identify and reproduce **gross sales, provider fees, VAT, other deductions, and net settlement** for every test batch | Downloadable report, provider invoice, and finance worksheet |
| Duplicate and missing records | No payment is recorded twice, and no approved transaction is missing from Shopify or provider reporting | Exception report showing zero unresolved items |
| Operational acceptance | Store Operations and Finance can complete the workflow without relying on undocumented steps | Signed pilot feedback and approval record |

## Maya Terminal workflow with Shopify POS

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the order in Shopify POS">
    Add the products and confirm the final amount on the Shopify POS device.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Process the same amount in Maya Terminal">
    Enter the amount and let the customer pay through an approved card, QR, or wallet method.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify the Maya approval">
    Confirm the approved status, exact amount, reference number, and payment method. Do not rely only on a customer screenshot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record the Shopify POS payment">
    Select the matching approved custom payment type, such as **Maya Terminal - Card** or **Maya Terminal - QR**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reconcile at closing">
    Compare Shopify POS payment-type totals with Maya's transaction and settlement reports. Investigate every difference before closing the day.
  </Step>
</Steps>
