# Shopify POS or Loyverse?

This page gives management the complete decision in one place. It explains what SixthGear gains, what it risks, what hardware is needed in the Philippines, how much the setup may cost, and what I recommend we do next.

<Info>
  **Decision in one sentence:** Keep Shopify as the online commerce system, keep Loyverse running for now, and conduct a controlled Shopify POS Lite pilot at one counter before deciding whether to retire Loyverse.
</Info>

## Why this decision matters

The live SixthGear website already depends on Shopify for products, collections, variants, inventory, customer accounts, carts, orders, and hosted checkout. Loyverse currently serves the physical-store POS workflow.

The decision is therefore not simply about choosing one POS application. It affects:

- The live ecommerce website
- Product and inventory ownership
- Online and in-store order records
- Staff workflow and training
- Receipt printing and barcode scanning
- Payment reconciliation
- Existing iMin or other POS hardware
- Subscription and replacement costs
- The amount of development work required

## The three realistic options

| Option | What it means | Main benefit | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Shopify online store \+ Shopify POS** | Shopify becomes the main system for online and physical-store commerce. | One product, inventory, customer, and order source. | Hardware cost and a less integrated local card-payment workflow. |
| **B. Shopify online store \+ Loyverse POS** | Shopify remains the ecommerce engine while Loyverse continues handling in-store sales. | Keeps the familiar POS and may reuse existing hardware. | Inventory and product records must be synchronized between two systems. |
| **C. Remove Shopify and keep Loyverse** | Loyverse remains the POS, but a different ecommerce backend must replace Shopify. | Removes the Shopify subscription. | Highest migration cost and risk because the current website commerce backend must be rebuilt. |

## My recommendation

My recommendation is to **keep Shopify as SixthGear’s online commerce platform**.

The current website already relies on Shopify for critical customer and business workflows. Removing Shopify would not be a simple account cancellation. We would need to replace product storage, collections, inventory, carts, customer accounts, orders, checkout, payment integration, and parts of the current storefront code. That would create a separate migration project with development cost, testing work, SEO risk, and possible disruption to online sales.

For the physical store, I do **not** recommend removing Loyverse immediately. I recommend a controlled Shopify POS Lite pilot at one counter first.

During the pilot, we should test:

1. Product and variant lookup
2. Barcode scanning
3. Receipt printing
4. Cash-drawer operation
5. External card-terminal recording
6. Inventory deduction
7. Returns and exchanges
8. Staff permissions
9. End-of-day reconciliation
10. Internet interruption and recovery

After two to four weeks of parallel checking, management can decide based on real store performance rather than feature lists.

<Check>
  If Shopify POS reduces duplicate work and keeps stock accurate without slowing down payment and reconciliation, retire Loyverse in a planned migration. If the payment workflow or existing hardware works much better in Loyverse, keep the hybrid setup and formalize inventory synchronization.
</Check>

## What I do not recommend

I do not recommend removing Shopify and expecting Loyverse alone to replace the online store.

Loyverse is mainly a POS, inventory, customer, and back-office platform. A separate ecommerce platform or custom integration would still be required. Removing Shopify would also require changes to the existing Next.js storefront and checkout architecture.

## Pros and cons

### Shopify online store \+ Shopify POS

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| One main product and inventory record | Supported hardware may cost more than generic Loyverse hardware |
| Online and physical orders can be reviewed in Shopify | Existing iMin hardware may not be suitable without testing |
| Less manual product duplication | Local card payments may still use an external terminal and manual confirmation |
| Easier channel publishing and stock control | POS Pro adds a separate location-based subscription when advanced features are needed |
| Existing Next.js storefront can remain | Staff need training and a controlled migration |
| POS Lite is included with a paid Shopify plan | Some Shopify-supported hardware must be sourced from third-party Philippine sellers |

### Shopify online store \+ Loyverse POS

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Keeps a familiar physical-store workflow | Shopify and Loyverse remain separate sources of inventory truth |
| Better chance of reusing existing iMin or low-cost Android hardware | Product, stock, returns, and adjustments need synchronization |
| Wider choice of inexpensive printers and peripherals | Integration or connector costs may apply |
| Lower immediate migration pressure | Troubleshooting involves more systems and service providers |
| Loyverse can continue while Shopify handles ecommerce | Staff may need duplicate product maintenance when synchronization fails |

### Remove Shopify and retain Loyverse

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- |
| Removes the Shopify subscription | Requires a replacement ecommerce platform or custom backend |
| Keeps the current physical-store POS | Product, collection, cart, account, order, and checkout integrations must be rebuilt |
| Gives management freedom to choose another online platform | Product and customer data migration is required |
|  | SEO URLs, redirects, analytics, and payment flows need migration and testing |
|  | Highest implementation risk and longest path to completion |

## Philippine payment reality

Shopify POS can still be used in the Philippines for product selection, inventory, customer records, cash sales, order creation, and receipt printing.

The important operational question is card processing. Before approving a full rollout, management must confirm whether the preferred card reader and payment provider can integrate directly with the current Shopify setup in the Philippines. When direct integration is not available, staff must process the card payment on the bank or payment-provider terminal, then record the result in Shopify POS using the approved payment method.

This means the sale can still be recorded in Shopify, but the card transaction and settlement details may remain in the external terminal or bank portal. The pilot must test whether that extra step is acceptable during busy store hours.

### Shopify-supported POS terminal format

Shopify does not currently provide an approved all-in-one POS computer for Philippine merchants. Shopify POS should run on a supported iPad or Android tablet, then connect to separately supported printers, scanners, and cash drawers.

Shopify's own POS Terminal and integrated card-reader hardware are limited to supported Shopify Payments countries, so they are not the recommended Philippine setup.

## Decision criteria for management

Use these questions after the pilot:

| Question | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| Does one sale update the correct Shopify inventory? | The online and physical stock records remain accurate after sales, returns, and cancellations. |
| Can staff complete a normal transaction quickly? | The checkout workflow is practical during busy periods. |
| Does receipt printing work consistently? | The printer reconnects reliably and produces the approved receipt layout. |
| Is the card-payment workflow acceptable? | Staff can process and reconcile the external terminal without confusion or duplicate recording. |
| Can the existing hardware be reused? | The exact device remains stable after repeated real transactions. |
| Are reports sufficient? | Management can reconcile cash, card, returns, discounts, and stock changes. |
| Is the total cost justified? | Reduced duplicate work and improved accuracy are worth the subscription and hardware cost. |

## Recommended implementation sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm the current environment">
    Record the Shopify plan, Loyverse subscription, exact iMin model, current printer, scanner, cash drawer, external terminal, and store-network setup.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose one pilot counter">
    Do not replace all existing equipment before the workflow is proven.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Purchase or borrow one supported printer">
    Use the Epson TM-m30III multi-interface model as the preferred pilot printer, subject to final seller and model verification.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use Shopify POS Lite first">
    Test the included POS features before paying for POS Pro.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run controlled transactions">
    Test cash, external card, discounts, returns, exchanges, receipts, scanner use, customer assignment, and stock deduction.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Compare records daily">
    Compare Shopify, Loyverse, the external payment terminal, and physical stock during the pilot.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review after two to four weeks">
    Measure transaction time, errors, inventory differences, reconciliation time, staff feedback, and support incidents.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approve one final direction">
    Choose full Shopify POS, the Shopify-and-Loyverse hybrid, or a stopped pilot. Record the approver, date, reason, budget, migration owner, and target date.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Final management recommendation

<Info>
  Keep Shopify for ecommerce. Keep Loyverse active while Shopify POS Lite is tested at one counter. Do not purchase a complete hardware rollout and do not cancel either system until the pilot proves inventory accuracy, receipt reliability, payment practicality, reporting quality, and staff readiness.
</Info>

This approach protects the current website, gives management real store evidence, and avoids committing SixthGear to expensive hardware or a high-risk migration before the operational benefits are proven.
